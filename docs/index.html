TGoat

TGoat is a bot that plays your OGame accounts. You follow and command it from a web page on your computer, from Telegram or from Discord.

  • The successor of TBot. TGoat does what TBot 3.6 did, and better: fewer requests to the game, decisions checked against the game's own rules, a Defender that keeps watching while you sleep.
  • It goes further than TBot ever did: lifeform research and settlement, Directives rewards, explorations, a web interface and a Discord bot of your own.
  • Nearly every behaviour is a setting, so it suits a careful first try as well as very advanced use. The setup assistant suggests settings that fit your account.
  • Your TBot settings are imported on the first start. See what changed, feature by feature.
  • It is an alpha: start with every feature off, on a secondary account (warnings).

What TGoat is

TGoat replaces TBot 3.6 and ogamed, the separate program TBot needed to reach the game. It is a new project, written from scratch in Go. Every feature was reviewed against the rules of the game and rebuilt where TBot's logic fell short.

One TGoat plays several accounts. Each account is an instance, with its own device, proxy and settings.

A note from the author

Many of TGoat's decisions come from my own reading of the game: its rules, how I understand the meta, and how a careful player would play. I did my best with all of that, and tested in the game what could be tested. Still, I cannot promise that every decision is optimal, or that each one makes sense in every situation on your account. When a choice does not suit you, the settings let you change it.

Zeus (zeus8000 on Discord)

Highlights

Safety and stealth

  • A Defender that never sleeps. At night it waits for the game's messages, such as a spy report, then checks. It frees a fleet slot for the fleet-save when all are taken, and warns you when it cannot read the attacks.
  • A small footprint. Fewer requests, one plausible PC per account, the account's proxy for everything and a human pace. How TGoat keeps its footprint small.

Smarter play

  • Raids that pay. AutoFarm simulates the battle against any defended target, checks the player is still inactive, and attacks only when the loot is worth the fuel. The AutoFarm page gives the reason for every target.
  • Expeditions and explorations. Fleets sized for the find the game allows, and the results of the last 24 hours and 7 days in the reports.
  • Ships it lacks, proposed. Pathfinders and cargo ships for expeditions, combat ships for defended targets, each with its cost and payback. Built when you accept.
  • An economy that reasons. Mines, lifeform buildings and research chosen by return on investment, with a reason for each choice.

A web interface

  • See your empire. A live map of your planets, moons, fleets and incoming attacks, with each feature's last pass and its reason.
  • Change settings safely. Forms with help for every setting, and a history you can restore.
  • Play by hand. The game opens inside TGoat, and manual mode keeps the bot out of your way.
  • Logs you can read. Time, instance › feature, then the message, with ⚠ and ✖ only when it matters and a colour per feature.

Take the tour of the web interface.

Easier setup

  • Your TBot settings, imported. On its first start, TGoat imports TBot's settings.json and reports what it dropped. Your old files are never changed.
  • Sign in with your browser. TGoat opens a browser window on the Gameforge login, you pass any captcha or two-factor yourself, and TGoat keeps the session. It works for a TGoat on a server too.
  • Signed updates. TGoat tells you about each new version and can install it at a quiet moment, only if the TGoat release key signed it.
  • A setup assistant. A few questions, then settings that fit your account, each with its reason. Nothing changes until you click Apply, and every change can be restored.
  • Profiles and history. Share settings between accounts, load a profile for a few hours, and restore an earlier version.

A Discord bot of your own

Alongside Telegram, you can create your own Discord bot and invite it to your server. It has every Telegram command, suggests values as you type, puts buttons on alerts and rings your phone on an attack. Only you, and the people you allow, can command it.

More control

  • A play mode switch. Human, RAGE or Custom: one switch sets how fast TGoat plays.
  • Directives. TGoat collects the rewards of your finished Directives (off by default).
  • Features on and off while it runs. Turn any feature on or off from the Map, the palette, Telegram or Discord, without touching the settings.
  • Priorities as lists. Drag features into order, with ties, instead of typing numbers.
  • Everything that changed from TBot, feature by feature.

More of the game

  • Lifeform research slots. When a slot opens, TGoat chooses its technology, the guaranteed one by default.
  • Lifeforms for your planets. Discovery can settle a lifeform on each planet that has none, Humans first (off by default).
  • What expeditions and explorations bring back. Read from the game's messages and shown in each feature's report.
  • Ready for Project Orion. TGoat keeps reading the game past Orion's anomalies and tells you when a reward waits. It never plays Orion.

Numbers that match the game

TGoat's formulas were checked against the official data of 30 universes and against 2,100 flights in the game. Its fuel figures match the game exactly. Coming from TBot, expect the Brain and AutoFarm to choose differently: TBot's numbers were often wrong.

How TGoat keeps its footprint small

The game sees a bot through what it sends: how many requests, how regular, from which address and from which browser. TGoat sends fewer requests than TBot did, spaces them like a player, and connects as one ordinary PC per account. This leaves less for Gameforge to notice. It does not make a bot invisible.

Fewer requests

The game's notification channel instead of polling

TGoat keeps the game's own notification channel open, as the game tab of a browser does. The channel marks no activity. At night the Defender sends no request until a message comes on it, such as a spy report. Every 20 to 40 minutes TGoat checks on the channel itself that it still works, without loading a game page.

It does not avoid this: each check after a message marks your current planet active, and an attack sent without a spy report first is not seen. See the Defender while the instance sleeps.

Probes from the galaxy view

AutoFarm spies with the galaxy view's spy button, as a player does, with the probe count of your game options: one request per target instead of a trip through the fleet pages. A 30-probe pass on a simulated game went from 309 requests to 40.

The whole empire in one view

The Empire view shows the stock, production, buildings, ships and defences of every planet in one page, and of every moon in another. AutoMine and the lifeform buildings start from it, and the boosters and the setup assistant reuse it, instead of opening each planet. Starting the Brain on a simulated game of 8 planets and 4 moons takes 47 reads instead of 104.

Every queue on one page

The production queue page lists the research queue and the building, lifeform and shipyard queues of every planet and moon, with their exact end times. TGoat reads it with the Empire view, so it knows when each build ends without visiting each planet.

Targets checked like the fleet page does

When you type a target on the fleet page, the game checks it (checkTarget). On universes whose flights skip empty or inactive systems, TGoat asks the same question without sending anything, so flight times and fuel are exact. The answer is kept 12 hours for each pair of systems and refreshed by every send; a universe that skips nothing is never asked.

Real loot from the return message

When an attack came back unseen, for example while TGoat was stopped, AutoFarm reads the game's "Return of a fleet" message for what it really brought home. It opens that tab at most once per pass, only when such an attack exists; as in the game, this marks those messages read, and none is deleted. The target's rest time and production then come from the real loot. To keep your espionage reports, turn off AutoFarm.DeleteReports: AutoFarm still reads them but deletes none.

Battles simulated on your computer

Before a raid, AutoFarm simulates the battle hundreds of times in TGoat itself. The simulation sends nothing: no request to the game, none to an outside simulator.

Like one ordinary player

A plausible PC for each account

Each account gets its own computer, drawn once from real market shares: Windows or macOS, Chrome or Edge, with a time zone and a language that fit the account. TGoat's connections carry that browser's network fingerprint (TLS handshake, HTTP/2 settings, headers), checked against the real Chrome. Your accounts do not look like one program, but they share your IP address unless each has its own proxy.

The account's proxy for everything

With a proxy set, every connection to Gameforge and to the game goes through it: the sign-in, the window of Sign in with your browser, the game pages and the notification channel. If the proxy cannot be used, TGoat waits for you to fix it and never connects without it. General.Proxy.LoginOnly (off by default) sends only the sign-in through it; Telegram, Discord, the captcha service and update checks never use it.

A human pace

In Human mode, the default, game requests come 1.5 to 8 seconds apart, with a 15 to 45 second break every 30 to 70 requests, and features react after a few minutes, not seconds. Breaks during the day are an option, off by default. RAGE drops every delay kept only for discretion: it plays much faster and is easy to spot (play mode).

What TGoat cannot hide

  • Every request to the game marks your current planet active, even a look at the event list. TGoat measured it in the game.
  • Gameforge does not say how it detects bots, so no setting is known to be safe.
  • Bots break OGame's rules and can get an account banned: use a secondary account.
